# US DOE Fundamentals Handbook: Mechanical Science - Centrifugal Pumps & Valves (DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93)

## 1. Centrifugal Pump Operational Principles
A centrifugal pump converts mechanical rotational energy into hydraulic flow and pressure. The rotating impeller imparts kinetic energy to the liquid, directing it outward into the volute casing. As fluid slows in the expanding volute area, velocity head is converted into static pressure head.

## 2. Key Performance Metrics
- **Volumetric Flow Rate (Q)**: Volume of liquid transferred per unit time (m3/h or gpm).
- **Total Dynamic Head (H)**: Net work done per unit weight of fluid (meters or feet).
- **Hydraulic Power (P_hyd)**: P_hyd = rho * g * Q * H, where rho is liquid density (kg/m3) and g is gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s2).
- **Pump Efficiency (eta)**: Ratio of hydraulic power delivered to shaft input power:
  eta = (P_hyd / P_shaft) * 100%
- **Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH)**: Difference between suction stagnation head and vapor pressure head. To prevent cavitation, NPSH_available must exceed NPSH_required by at least 0.6 m margin.

## 3. Common Failure Modes in Rotating Equipment
- **Cavitation**: Collapse of vapor bubbles causing pitting erosion on impeller vanes and elevated high-frequency vibration.
- **Unbalance & Misalignment**: Produces dominant 1X and 2X rotational speed vibration peaks.
- **Bearing Fatigue & Spalling**: Manifests as bearing pass frequency harmonics (BPFO, BPFI).
